We make furniture to be lived with for a long time.

Morrow started with one sofa and a simple rule: if we wouldn’t keep it for twenty years, we don’t sell it. Everything we make can be repaired, recovered or passed on.

How it started

In 2018 Ana and Daniel Morrow moved apartments for the fourth time in six years. Their sofa, bought new two years earlier, didn’t survive the stairs. The frame had been stapled together from particleboard.

Daniel had trained as a joiner, so they built a replacement in their garage: an oak frame, proper springs, and a cover that zipped off. Friends asked for one. Then friends of friends. That sofa became the Alder, and it’s still the thing we’re proudest of.

  1. 2018

    A sofa in a garage

    Ana and Daniel Morrow build their first sofa in a Portland garage because they couldn’t find one that would outlast a move.

  2. 2019

    The Alder

    The first Alder sofa goes on sale. The 40 we make sell out in a weekend, mostly to neighbours.

  3. 2021

    Our own workshop

    We open a workshop in Hickory, North Carolina, with six upholsterers who’d spent decades in the trade.

  4. 2023

    Showrooms

    Portland, Brooklyn and Chicago. Places to sit on things before you buy them.

  5. 2025

    Repair, not replace

    We start selling every part separately, from covers to legs, and collect old pieces for refurbishing.

  6. 2026

    Today

    214 people, 6 workshops we work with closely, and more than 60,000 homes.

What we won’t compromise on

  • Solid materials

    Oak, walnut, stone, wool and linen. No particleboard frames, no veneer pretending to be solid wood, and we say so on every product page.

  • Repair before replace

    Every part is sold separately and our workshop refurbishes pieces people send back. A sofa should see a few decades, not a few moves.

  • A fair price, shown plainly

    We sell directly, so there’s no showroom markup on markup. Sales happen twice a year, and the price you see is the price everyone pays.
